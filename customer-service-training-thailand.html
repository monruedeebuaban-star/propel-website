The problem you recognize

You've set the service standard. Does every frontline moment live up to it?

Service standards live in a handbook and a one-time briefing. On a busy floor, in front of a frustrated customer, behavior comes down to habit, confidence and judgment.

Standards that stay on paperWritten service values don't translate into consistent frontline behavior.

Recovery that escalatesStaff freeze or get defensive when things go wrong, and small issues become complaints.

Inconsistent experienceService quality swings by person, by shift and by mood.

Great service isn't taught once. It's the behavior your team defaults to when the pressure is on.
The shift that matters

From knowing the service standard — to living it under pressure.

Real service development shows up in the next difficult customer moment, not the training slide.

Before
  • Follows the script until it breaks
